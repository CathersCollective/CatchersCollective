# Catcher's Collective Curriculum Workspace

This thread is the dedicated workspace for planning, structuring, writing, and implementing the Catcher's Collective catching curriculum.

## Founder Story and Curriculum Point of View

Josh Crouch did not grow up as a catcher-only player. In high school, he was a third baseman committed to Florida International University in Miami. After attending pre-draft workouts as an infielder, a Los Angeles Dodgers scout told him he needed to start catching. That conversation changed his path.

Josh decommitted from FIU and committed to State College of Florida, a junior college where he could learn how to catch. He spent two years there developing the position from the ground up, then committed to the University of Central Florida. After two years at UCF, he was drafted in the 11th round by the Detroit Tigers in 2021.

Since being drafted, Josh has spent six years in professional baseball between the Detroit Tigers and Los Angeles Angels organizations and played up to the Double-A level. Along the way, he has been around high-level players, coaches, pitchers, coordinators, and development systems that shaped the coach he is today.

This curriculum should carry that story:

- It is built for catchers who need a clear path, not random drills.
- It teaches the athlete inside the catcher because Josh came to the position as an athlete first.
- It respects that catching can be learned, developed, and refined through intentional work.
- It blends infield athleticism, catcher-specific skill, game leadership, pitcher management, and professional standards.
- It should feel practical, honest, and earned, not theoretical.

Curriculum voice:

- Coach from experience.
- Explain the why behind the movement.
- Give players language they can use in games.
- Make each age range feel like the next step in a real development journey.
- Keep the standard high while making the work understandable.

## Course Tracks

### 8-12 Foundations: The Foundation Code

Purpose: Build a confident young catcher who understands the position, moves well, and has a repeatable foundation before the game speeds up.

Core focus:

- Athletic development: Build coordination, balance, body control, hip mobility, foot speed, and basic strength patterns so young catchers can move safely and confidently.
  - Mobility
  - Stability
  - Arm care: daily routines, longevity and durability, standout-from-the-rest habits, in-season plans, catcher-specific recovery systems, Indian clubs, grip strength, and athletic Nerf ball tosses.
  - Agility and coordination: train the athlete inside the catcher, include ground balls, and use agility ladder, skills ball, tennis ball, quick hands, water bags, water balls, wall ball, hip halo, ankle boards, and cone drills.
- Nutrition and habits: Introduce simple routines around hydration, fueling before training, sleep, effort, attitude, and taking care of gear.
- Receiving: Teach stance, target presentation, quiet glove work, basic pitch tracking, and how to make the pitcher feel comfortable throwing to them.
  - Setup
  - Angles
  - Targeting
  - Stances
  - Load
  - Targeting extremes
  - Pocket awareness
  - Timing
  - Body awareness and control
  - Low pitch
  - High pitch
  - Corners
  - One-knee and traditional integration
  - Strike stealing
  - Keeping strikes strikes
  - Turning balls into strikes
  - Soft hands
  - Be athletic
- Blocking: Build comfort getting to the ground, creating a wall, keeping the ball in front, recovering quickly, and removing fear from the skill.
  - Mentality
  - Angles
  - Hip mobility
  - Chest positioning
  - Hockey goalie concepts
  - Anticipation
  - Lateral recovery
  - Deadening balls
  - Controlling movement patterns
  - Controlled anticipated athleticism
  - Not a drop-and-block mentality
  - Challenging ourselves in practice
  - Random game-like drills
- Transfers and footwork: Teach clean catch-to-hand movement, simple footwork patterns, balance, rhythm, and direction before adding speed.
- Throwing: Build throwing accuracy, arm care awareness, proper direction to the target, and confidence making throws to bases.
  - Footwork efficiency
  - Transfer speed and accuracy
  - Arm path with no slop
  - Direction
  - Lower-half sequencing
  - Different arm angles
  - Bouncing the ball to second base
  - Pop time development
  - Accuracy under pressure
  - Back picks and when to use them
  - Modified pitchouts
  - Pitchouts
  - Vault leads and containing them
  - Handling the running game
  - First and third
  - Bunts
  - Throwing on the run
  - Throwing to targets and moving targets
  - Eyes-closed throwing
- Game IQ: Teach counts, outs, runners, basic signs, force plays, simple bunt coverage ideas, and what the catcher should be thinking before each pitch.

Readiness outcome:

- The catcher can receive, block, throw, communicate, and lead at a youth level with clear routines and growing confidence.

### 13-15 Development: The Field General Blueprint

Purpose: Help middle school and early high school catchers handle the transition to a faster, more physical, more tactical game.

Core focus:

- Athletic development: Develop mobility, strength, speed, agility, body control, and catcher-specific conditioning for athletes entering faster and more physical baseball.
  - Mobility
  - Stability
  - Arm care: daily routines, longevity and durability, standout-from-the-rest habits, in-season plans, catcher-specific recovery systems, Indian clubs, grip strength, and athletic Nerf ball tosses.
  - Agility and coordination: train the athlete inside the catcher, include ground balls, and use agility ladder, skills ball, tennis ball, quick hands, water bags, water balls, wall ball, hip halo, ankle boards, and cone drills.
- Nutrition and recovery: Build better hydration, fueling, sleep, warm-up, cooldown, and recovery habits as training volume and game demands increase.
- Receiving: Improve glove presentation, pitch tracking, body quietness, low-pitch handling, and the ability to receive different pitch types with consistency.
- Blocking: Progress from basic blocking to better reads, angles, lateral movement, recovery footwork, and controlling where the ball ends up.
  - Mentality
  - Angles
  - Hip mobility
  - Chest positioning
  - Hockey goalie concepts
  - Anticipation
  - Lateral recovery
  - Deadening balls
  - Controlling movement patterns
  - Controlled anticipated athleticism
  - Not a drop-and-block mentality
  - Challenging ourselves in practice
  - Random game-like drills
- Transfers and footwork: Clean up exchange timing, foot direction, rhythm, and lower-half efficiency so throws become quicker without becoming rushed.
- Throwing: Improve accuracy, carry, arm path, release consistency, and decision-making on steals, back picks, bunts, and recovery throws.
  - Footwork efficiency
  - Transfer speed and accuracy
  - Arm path with no slop
  - Direction
  - Lower-half sequencing
  - Different arm angles
  - Bouncing the ball to second base
  - Pop time development
  - Accuracy under pressure
  - Back picks and when to use them
  - Modified pitchouts
  - Pitchouts
  - Vault leads and containing them
  - Handling the running game
  - First and third
  - Bunts
  - Throwing on the run
  - Throwing to targets and moving targets
  - Eyes-closed throwing
- Game management and IQ: Develop awareness of counts, runners, outs, hitter tendencies, pitcher strengths, defensive situations, and the catcher's pre-pitch plan.
- Leadership: Teach mound visits, body language, pitcher encouragement, communication with coaches and infielders, and how to stay steady when the game speeds up.
- Film and self-evaluation: Introduce simple video review habits so athletes can identify what they did well, what needs work, and what to focus on next.

Readiness outcome:

- The catcher can manage a faster game, support a pitching staff, control the run game, and show the habits needed for high school competition.

## Pillar Builder

Use this structure to build every pillar inside each age range. Keep the language simpler for younger players and add more detail, ownership, and decision-making as the athlete gets older.

Build order:

1. Define the pillar goal.
2. List Josh's non-negotiable teaching points.
3. Add drills and progressions from simple to game-like.
4. Add at-home work or weekly habits.
5. Add video or film tasks.
6. Add checkpoints that prove the athlete owns the skill.
7. Decide what belongs in the public course, what belongs in private coaching, and what should stay as coach-only teaching notes.

Every pillar should connect back to Josh's story: athlete first, catcher developed through intention, and professional standards layered over time.

Pillar build fields:

- Pillar goal: What the athlete should understand or be able to do by the end.
- Key teaching points: The main ideas, cues, or standards Josh wants taught.
- Drill library: The drills, progressions, or practice blocks that teach the pillar.
- At-home work: Simple homework, routines, or habits the athlete can repeat.
- Video or film: What to record, review, or study.
- Checkpoint: How Josh knows the athlete is ready to progress.

## Build Roadmap

### Phase 1: Finish the curriculum blueprint

Goal: Make the course clear before filming.

- Lock the four course names and age ranges.
- Finish the pillar builder for one age range at a time.
- Start with 8-12 Foundations because it will force the language to stay simple and teachable.
- For every pillar, fill in:
  - Pillar goal
  - Key teaching points
  - Drill library
  - At-home work
  - Video or film
  - Checkpoint
- Decide which pieces are public course lessons, private coaching add-ons, and coach-only notes.

### Phase 2: Build the first minimum viable course

Goal: Launch one strong course before building all four.

Recommended first course: 8-12 Foundations: The Foundation Code.

Why this course first:

- It is the simplest to explain to parents.
- It creates the entry point for the entire academy.
- It builds trust before families buy advanced products.
- It can connect naturally to private lessons, camps, and group training.

Recommended first-course structure:

- Welcome from Josh and his catching story.
- How to use the course.
- Athletic development.
- Nutrition and habits.
- Receiving.
- Blocking.
- Transfers and footwork.
- Throwing.
- Game IQ.
- Final checklist and next steps.

### Phase 3: Film in small batches

Goal: Avoid filming a giant course before the teaching order is proven.

- Film one pillar at a time.
- For each lesson, capture:
  - Josh explaining the why.
  - Demonstration from the front and side.
  - Common mistake.
  - Correction cue.
  - Drill progression.
  - At-home version.
  - Checkpoint.
- Keep lessons short enough for players and parents to actually use.

### Phase 4: Launch as a beta

Goal: Test the course with real families before making the whole academy.

- Invite a small beta group of local families.
- Give them discounted or early access.
- Ask what was clear, what was confusing, and what helped the athlete train.
- Use feedback to tighten the language, lesson order, and drills.
- Collect testimonials and before/after development notes.

### Phase 5: Add coaching layers

Goal: Turn the academy into a development ecosystem, not just a course library.

Potential offers:

- Course only.
- Course plus monthly video review.
- Course plus group Zoom or live Q&A.
- Course plus private lesson package.
- Full academy membership with all courses and seasonal updates.

### Phase 6: Build the advanced tracks

Goal: Let the curriculum mature with the athlete.

Recommended order:

1. 8-12 Foundations: The Foundation Code.
2. 13-15 Development: The Field General Blueprint.
3. 16-18 High School Prep: The Prospect Signal.
4. 19-22 College: Behind the Plate Masterclass.

## Platform Recommendation

Best fit right now: Kajabi.

Why Kajabi fits this curriculum:

- The Catcher's Collective needs courses, coaching offers, memberships, community, email, landing pages, checkout, automations, and future upsells.
- The curriculum is built around Josh's story and expertise, so the platform needs to support a full business ecosystem, not only host videos.
- Kajabi can start with one course and later expand into coaching programs, memberships, communities, digital downloads, newsletters, and higher-touch offers.
- The public website can stay as the brand/SEO home while Kajabi handles the paid academy, checkout, email, and student experience.

Recommended setup:

- Keep `catcherscollective.com` as the public marketing site.
- Use Kajabi for the paid academy.
- Link the website buttons to Kajabi checkout or Kajabi landing pages when the first course is ready.
- Start with one product: 8-12 Foundations.
- Add video review, membership, and advanced courses after the first product proves demand.

Other platforms considered:

- Teachable: good if the goal is a simple course storefront and fast video-course launch.
- Thinkific: good for structured courses, lead magnets, communities, memberships, and bundles.
- LearnWorlds: strong for interactive learning, assessments, white-label course experience, and possible mobile-app direction.

Current recommendation:

- Use Kajabi if the vision is a coaching business and academy ecosystem.
- Use Teachable if the priority is simplest course launch.
- Use Thinkific if the priority is structured courses and communities with a simpler LMS feel.
- Use LearnWorlds if the priority is a more interactive learning product and branded learning experience.
